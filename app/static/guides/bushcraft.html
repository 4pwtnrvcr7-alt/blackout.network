🚦 Start here: what matters first

3️⃣ The Rule of 3s: how long you can go without…

⏱ First 72 hours: do these in order

Do it in order.Food comes last. You have time.

1. 🦺 Get away from danger

🔥Fire, damaged buildingsMove away
⚡Downed power linesStay far back
🌊Flood waterStay out: holes, live wires, sewage
👃Gas smellLeave. No switches. Call from outside
☠️Generator, grill or stove indoorsNever: carbon monoxide kills
🩹Check peopleCall 911. Life threats first

2. 💧 Water: 1 gallon per person per day

🛁Fill containers and tubs before the storm
🥤Don't ration: drink what you need today
♨️Boil-water notice? Treat all drinking water

3. ⛺ Shelter, warmth or cooling

🏠Sound building, or a public shelter if told to evacuate
☀️HeatShade, airflow, wet cloth on skin, rest in the hottest hours
❄️ColdLayers, blankets, one warm room

4. Information & contact

Battery or hand-crank NOAA Weather Radio + AM/FM
💬Text, don't callTexts get through busy networks
🔋Save battery: low-power mode, dim screen
Out-of-area contact + meeting place

6. 🎒 Go-bag check

💧Water + treatment (filter, tablets, unscented bleach + dropper)
🥫3 days of food + manual can opener
🔦Flashlight, spare batteries, NOAA radio
🩹First-aid kit, meds (7 days), glasses
📄ID, insurance, medical info in a waterproof bag; cash

All outdoor water must be treated.Boil if you can. Bleach or tablets if you can't. Filter cloudy water first.

🔎 Where to find water

🏠Inside firstStored water · water heater tank (power/gas off first, drain from the bottom valve) · ice cubes · toilet tank (not the bowl; not with chemical cleaners)
🌧Outside: treat it allRain in clean containers · springs · streams · lakes. Moving, clear water beats stagnant.

⛺ Shelter

High, dry, nothing overhead, off the ground.The ground steals body heat fast, even in mild weather.

Pick the spot

⛰️High, well-drained ground
🌊Not a dry wash, ditch or flood zone
🌳Look up: no dead trees or hanging branches
🐜No ant mounds, nests, animal trails, standing water
💧Near (not next to) water, out of the wind

🛏 Insulate from the ground

Use a pad, cardboard, or a 15+ cm (6+ in) bed of dry leaves, pine straw or palm fronds.

🍂 Build a debris hut in 4 steps

Ridge pole on a forked stick, hip height, sloping to the ground

Lean sticks along both sides like ribs

Pile dry leaves very thick (an arm's length); stuff the inside too

Keep it small: just big enough for your body

🟦 Tarp: lean-to or A-frame

🪢Tie a ridge line between two trees
⛺Drape over (A-frame) or tie one edge low (lean-to) into the wind
📌Stake tight; slope so rain runs off
🌴 Hot-humid climates (Florida, Gulf Coast, tropics) Shade and airflow matter more than warmth most of the year. Pitch the tarp high with open sides, keep off wet ground, use a bug net, long sleeves and repellent (mosquitoes carry disease). Check for ticks and fire ants; watch for snakes when moving debris. Summer afternoon lightning: avoid lone tall trees and open high ground.

🔥 Fire

Gather everything before you strike.Tinder → kindling → fuel. Dead branches still on trees are usually drier than ground wood.

☀️ Heat + 🪵 Fuel + 💨 Oxygen
Take one away and it goes out.

Tinder → kindling (pencil-thick) → fuel (wrist-thick+). Add gently; don't smother it.

🌾 Good tinderDry grass, cattail fluff, birch bark, dry pine needles, cotton balls with petroleum jelly, dryer lint.

🔦 Ways to light it

🧯 Fire safety

Clear 3 m (10 ft) to bare soil · use an existing fire ring · check burn bans

👀Never leave itKeep water or sand nearby
💧Put it outDrown → stir → feel for heat
☠️ Never run generators, grills, charcoal or camp stoves indoors, in garages or tents. Carbon monoxide is invisible and odorless.

Generator outside, 20+ ft from doors and windows · battery CO alarms inside

🍲 Food

Stored food first. Wild food last.Keep 3 days (ideally 2 weeks) of food that needs no cooking.
🧊➡️❄️➡️🥫Perishables → frozen → canned/dry
🌊Throw outFood touched by flood water
🌡️Throw outAnything above 40°F (4°C) for 2+ hours

🎣 Fishing

🪝Line, small hooks, sinkers, bobber
🪱Bait: worms, insects, bread
🌅Early morning & evening, near logs, docks, weed edges
📋License + limits (Florida: FWC). Cook fish thoroughly

🪤 Trapping: tightly regulated (Florida: FWC). In a true emergency, snares or fish traps are a last resort: know the law first and never trap protected species.

⚠️ Foraging: if in doubt, don't. Many deadly plants look like edible ones. Only eat plants you can identify with certainty from multiple reliable sources or an expert. The Army "universal edibility test" is slow, risky and no shortcut: it doesn't protect you from the most dangerous plants or mushrooms. Never eat wild mushrooms unless an expert identifies them.

🧂 Preserve it

☀️DryThin strips, sun or low heat, until brittle
🧂SaltPack in plenty of salt; rinse/soak before cooking
💨SmokeLow hardwood smoke for hours; hot-smoke to a safe internal temp
🌴 Hot-humid climatesHeat and humidity spoil food fast: preserve or eat it quickly. Coconuts, citrus and saw palmetto berries are known foods, but harvest only where legal and with permission. Shellfish can carry red-tide toxins: check FWC/health advisories.

🩹 First aid

🚨 What to do, in order

🩸 Stop the bleed: escalate until it stops

Press hard with cloth or gauze. Don't lift to check.

Deep wound on an arm, leg or junction? Pack it tight and keep pressing.

Pressure won't stop limb bleeding? Tourniquet 2–3 in above the wound (not on a joint). Tighten until it stops. Note the time.

Take a Stop the Bleed class.

🌡 Heat illness: know the difference

🧼 Wounds & 🔥 burns

🧼CleanWash hands; rinse with treated water for several minutes
🩹CoverClean dressing, change daily
🔴Infection → get careSpreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks, fever
🚿Burn: cool 20 minCool running water. No ice, butter or creams. Cover loosely, non-stick
🚑Burn → 911Large, deep, face/hands/genitals, or electrical

🧰 Med kit

🩹Gauze pads & rolls, bandages, tape, elastic bandage
🎗️Tourniquet (CAT/SOF-T) + pressure bandage
🧤Gloves, trauma shears, tweezers, thermometer
🧴Antiseptic wipes, saline/clean water
🥤Oral rehydration salts, emergency blanket, cold packs
💊OTC pain/fever, antihistamine, anti-diarrheal (label only)
📋Your prescriptions + list of meds & allergies
💊 Take prescriptions exactly as your doctor/pharmacist directs and OTC medicines only as the label says. This guide gives no dosing advice.

🧭 Navigation

Carry a paper map. Know your "catching feature".A road or river that stops you if you overshoot. Download area map PDFs in the Maps tab ahead of time.

✋StopStay put. Don't wander.
🧠ThinkHow did you get here?
👀ObserveLandmarks, map, compass, sun
PlanMove only with a plan, or stay and signal

🧭 Orient the map in 3 steps

📐
Compass flat, away from metal and phones. Edge along a north–south grid line.

🔄
Turn map + compass together until the needle points to map north. Adjust for declination (check NOAA's value for your location).

🏞
Match roads, lakes, power lines and towers to the map. Tick off landmarks as you pass.

Reading the mapLearn the legend: roads, water, contour lines (land shape) and scale.

🌿 Plants

Never touch, eat or use a plant you can't identify with 100% certainty.Confirm with multiple reliable sources or a local expert (UF/IFAS Extension, a botanist).
⚠️ Plant ID mistakes can kill. This is education, not medical advice: "traditionally used for" means historical/folk use, not proven treatment. Suspected poisoning: Poison Control 1-800-222-1222 (24/7, free), or 911 if unconscious, seizing or struggling to breathe.

🌱 Beginner medicinal plants (traditionally used for…)

🍃 Broadleaf plantain Plantago major

👁 How to spot itlawn "weed"; low rosette of oval leaves, strong parallel veins (stringy when torn), pencil-like seed spikes.

Traditional use: crushed-leaf poultice for bites, stings, minor skin irritation.

🌼 Yarrow Achillea millefolium

👁 How to spot itfeathery, finely divided fern-like leaves; flat-topped clusters of small white flowers.

Traditional use: minor cuts (styptic).

⚠️ Deadly carrot-family look-alikes (water hemlock): umbrella-shaped flower clusters on hollow, purple-blotched stems. Skin reactions if allergic to ragweed/daisies.

🌳 Willow bark Salix spp.

👁 How to spot itlong narrow leaves, often near water. Inner bark has salicin (aspirin-like).

Traditional use: pain, fever.

⚠️ Same cautions as aspirin: not for children or teens (Reye's syndrome), aspirin allergy, bleeding disorders, ulcers, blood thinners, or pregnancy.

🫐 Elderberry Sambucus nigra ssp. canadensis

👁 How to spot itshrub, compound leaves (5–11 toothed leaflets), flat clusters of tiny white flowers, then purple-black berries.

Traditional use: colds/flu as a cooked syrup.

⚠️ Only fully ripe berries, only cooked. Raw/unripe berries, leaves, stems, bark and roots are toxic.

🌻 Dandelion Taraxacum officinale

👁 How to spot ittoothed leaves in a rosette; one yellow flower per hollow stem; milky sap.

Use: leaves, flowers, roots edible; traditionally a mild diuretic and digestive bitter.

⚠️ Avoid sprayed lawns/roadsides. May interact with some medications.

🪴 Aloe vera

👁 How to spot itthick, spiky-edged succulent leaves with clear gel.

Traditional use: gel on skin for minor burns and sunburn.

⚠️ Don't eat it: the yellow latex under the skin is a strong laxative.

💜 American beautyberry Callicarpa americana

👁 How to spot itFlorida native shrub; bright purple berry clusters hugging the stems in fall.

Traditional use: crushed leaves rubbed on skin as insect repellent (test a small area first).

⚠️ Berries are not a good food raw.

☠️ Toxic plants: know these (southeastern US examples)

Deadly / highly toxicRash / irritant

☂️ Water hemlock Deadly

👁 How to spot itwet ditches/marshes; umbrella-shaped white flower clusters; hollow, often purple-streaked stems; chambered root.

A small bite can cause fatal seizures. Avoid all carrot-family look-alikes.

🌸 Oleander Deadly

👁 How to spot itlandscape shrub; long narrow leathery leaves; pink/white/red flowers.

All parts highly toxic to people and pets (heart poison). Never use for skewers or burn it.

🌱 Castor bean Deadly

👁 How to spot itlarge star-shaped, often reddish leaves; spiny pods; mottled bean-like seeds.

Seeds contain ricin: deadly if chewed.

🔴 Rosary pea Deadly

👁 How to spot itinvasive vine, feathery compound leaves; shiny red seeds with a black spot.

Contains abrin: a chewed seed can be fatal. Never use for jewelry.

🍏 Manchineel Deadly

👁 How to spot itrare coastal South Florida/Keys tree; small green apple-like fruit; often signposted.

Fruit, sap, even rain dripping off it burn skin and eyes. Never shelter under or burn it.

🌙 Jimsonweed Datura Deadly

👁 How to spot itlarge white/purple trumpet flowers; foul-smelling leaves; spiny pods.

All parts toxic: hallucinations, racing heart, death.

🌺 Coral bean Erythrina herbacea Toxic

👁 How to spot itnative shrub; tall spikes of red tubular flowers; pods with bright red seeds.

Seeds are poisonous.

🟠 Lantana Toxic

👁 How to spot itshrub; clusters of small yellow/orange/pink flowers.

Green (unripe) berries toxic; leaves toxic to livestock and pets. Don't eat the berries.

🍂 Poison ivy Rash

👁 How to spot it"leaves of three, let it be"; middle leaflet on a longer stalk; hairy-rootlet vine or shrub.

Urushiol rash. Wash with soap + cool water within minutes; wash clothes and tools. Never burn it.

🍁 Atlantic poison oak Rash

👁 How to spot itlow shrub; three oak-like lobed leaflets, fuzzy undersides; sandy pinelands.

Same urushiol rash as poison ivy.

🌿 Poison sumac Rash

👁 How to spot itswamps/wet areas; 7–13 smooth leaflets on red stems; drooping white/gray berries.

Very strong urushiol rash.

🌶 Brazilian pepper Irritant

👁 How to spot itinvasive across Central/South Florida; 3–13 leaflets, peppery smell crushed; red berry clusters in winter.

Poison-ivy relative: sap and blooms cause rash and breathing irritation.

📞 Save Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222. Photo the plant (or bag a sample) for responders. More: UF/IFAS Extension (EDIS).
